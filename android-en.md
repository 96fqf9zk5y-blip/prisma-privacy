## Game data on your device

Prisma Daily does not require its own user account. Puzzles, progress, notes,
hint credits, playing times, Daily streaks, learning progress, achievements,
statistics and settings are stored locally in the app’s private storage.
The app does not upload these saved games to a server operated by us.
We have not added our own analytics or cloud synchronisation service.

You can delete local data through Android’s app settings or uninstall the app.
Doing so removes your local game progress. A purchase made through Google Play
remains associated with your Google account and can be restored.

## Reminders and widget

If you choose, a local notification reminds you about the daily puzzle.
You enable this feature yourself and can turn it off in the app or Android
settings. The reminder time is stored locally; this feature uses no push
service and sends no advertising messages. An optional home screen widget
shows your local Daily status. People who can see your unlocked screen may
also be able to see this information.

## Full-version purchase

Purchases and payments are handled through Google Play. The app receives the
product identifier, purchase status and technical proof of purchase to unlock
the full version, acknowledge purchases and restore them. The last confirmed
unlock status is stored locally. The app does not receive credit card or bank
account details. Google processes payment and account information under its
own privacy information. Billing records required by law may be retained
after you uninstall the app. If you have been given an unlock code, the app
checks it locally and also stores the resulting unlock status on your device.

The Google Play Billing library also processes technical diagnostic data to
operate and troubleshoot purchase and restoration features and sends this
data to Google. This can happen when the app starts, regardless of whether
you allow advertising or make a purchase. The local age-group choice does
not turn off this purchase management.

## Advertising and consent

Before loading advertising, the app asks whether you are at least 18 years
old. Only your choice is stored on your device, not your date of birth.
If you select “Under 18” or choose not to answer, no requests are sent to
AdMob or UMP. You can change your choice in settings.

The free version uses Google AdMob. You can choose to watch an advertising
video for extra hints. Ads may also appear occasionally after a completed
puzzle. Once the full version is unlocked, the app does not request further
ads; purchase restoration remains available.

We use Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP) to manage your choices. Where
required, a dialog appears before advertising with options to consent, reject
or adjust your preferences. It also explains the participating advertising
partners and purposes. Your choice is saved and taken into account when
requesting ads. In the app’s settings, “Ad privacy options” lets you reopen
the available choices and withdraw consent. Withdrawal applies to future
processing. The free game features remain available without consenting to
personalised advertising. For adult users, UMP may also update existing
privacy settings after the ad-free full version has been unlocked.

Depending on consent and configuration, Google’s advertising SDK processes
your IP address, which may be used to estimate your approximate location;
device identifiers such as the Android advertising ID; interactions with
the app and ads; and technical diagnostic data. These are used to deliver
ads, measure their performance and prevent abuse. Data is encrypted in
transit. The app does not request GPS location permission.

Rejecting personalised advertising does not necessarily mean that no data
is transmitted: where limited advertising is permitted, technical data may
still be processed. You can also delete or reset your Android advertising
ID in your device settings.

Further information: [Google’s privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy?hl=en)
and [data handling by the Google Mobile Ads SDK](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure).

## Purposes and legal bases

Processing for requested game features, purchases and restoration is used
to perform the contract with you (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). Advertising purposes
that require consent are based on your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR).
Where legally permitted, necessary technical diagnostics and security
processing serve our legitimate interest in stable, secure operation free
from abuse (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Statutory retention obligations are based
on Article 6(1)(c) GDPR.

Storing or accessing information on your device is also subject to
Section 25 of Germany’s TDDDG. Consent is obtained where no legal exception
for strictly necessary operations applies.

## Recipients, international processing and retention

Recipients are Google and the advertising partners listed in the consent
dialog for the purposes you select. Processing outside the European Economic
Area is possible. Information about Google’s international processing and
safeguards is available in [Google’s privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy?hl=en).
Information about partners is accessible through the consent dialog.

Local app data remains stored until it is deleted. Retention by Google and
advertising partners depends on the purpose, your settings and legal
requirements; their respective privacy information describes their practices.
Deleting the app does not automatically delete data stored externally.

## Contact requests and your rights

If you contact us by email, we process your message and contact details to
handle your request. Messages that are no longer needed are deleted unless
retention is required.

Subject to the GDPR, you have rights of access, rectification, erasure,
restriction of processing, data portability and objection. You may withdraw
consent at any time with effect for the future. Please use the contact
address above. For data that Google processes as an independent controller,
you can also use Google’s privacy tools. You may complain to a data protection
supervisory authority, particularly the State Commissioner for Data Protection
of Lower Saxony, Germany.
